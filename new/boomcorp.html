Overview

The game

You have been recruited by Boom Corp., the galaxy's premier provider of mine detection services. Each room is an 8 × 8 floor of tiles with eight explosives buried somewhere under it. You carry two tools: a scanner that tells you how many explosives touch the tile in front of you, and a blaster that clears a tile. Clear every safe tile and you win. Clear the wrong one and the room finds out what was under it.

I made it alone for the Druid Mechanics Game Jam 2025, from an empty project to a released build in nine days, and it won the jam.

Try the idea

The same puzzle in two dimensions: an 8 × 8 room with 8 explosives. Click to clear a tile, right-click to mark one. Your first tile is always safe here; in the game it isn't.

Systems

What I built

01

The grid

BP_TileManager spawns the floor, picks which tiles hide explosives at random each run, and answers the two questions the rest of the game asks: how many explosives touch this tile, and how far away is the nearest one.

02

Scanner and blaster

The player swaps between two tools with animated transitions. Right-click scans: a screen on the scanner model itself shows the count for the tile under the laser sight. Left-click fires a projectile that clears the tile it hits.

03

Proximity beeper

The scanner beeps faster as you get closer to an explosive. The interval comes from the distance, in tiles, to the nearest one, so you can feel your way around the room before you commit to a shot.

04

Consequences

A safe tile breaks apart with a Niagara burst and leaves a hole. A live one triggers a radial physics impulse that throws the room's props around, a camera shake, and the game-over screen.

05

Toon shader

The look is a post-process material: cel shading plus an outline pass that detects edges from both scene depth and surface normals, with line thickness adjusted by distance and viewing angle so outlines hold up close and far.

06

Everything around the game

Title screen, intro cutscene, an onboarding tutorial that a save file marks as seen so it only plays once, HUD, win and lose screens, credits, music and UI sounds.

Step by step

One turn

  1. Listen

    The beeper speeds up near an explosive.

  2. Scan

    Right-click a tile to read how many explosives touch it.

  3. Deduce

    Cross-reference the numbers, as in Minesweeper.

  4. Shoot

    Left-click clears a tile. There is no undo.

  5. Win or boom

    Clear all 56 safe tiles, or hit one of the 8.

The hard part

From concept to completion in nine days

  1. Problem

    A nine-day jam, alone, covering design, programming, art direction and sound. The risk in a jam is a clever idea that never becomes a finished game.

  2. Approach

    I got the core loop playable by the end of day two and treated everything after it as polish on something that already worked. I posted progress publicly every day, which kept each day tied to a specific milestone.

  3. Result

    A complete, published game ahead of the deadline, and first place. The host's verdict: "Absolutely awesome game. Exceptional work."

Note

After the jam: v1.2

The jam build used fully dynamic lighting with Lumen. For a single static room that was wasted cost, so v1.2 bakes all the lighting, replaces temporal super resolution with FXAA and strips the plugins the game never used. The download dropped from 430 MB to 307 MB and the game runs on weaker hardware.
